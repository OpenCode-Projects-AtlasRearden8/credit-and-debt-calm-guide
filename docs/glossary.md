# Glossary

*Every term in this guide, defined like a friend would — one or two sentences, and which chapter uses it.*

## A–C

**1099-C** — The IRS form a creditor files when it "cancels" debt, generally $600 or more of it. Forgiven debt is generally counted as taxable income unless an exception applies. Used in [chapter 07](07-spotting-credit-and-debt-scams.md)'s settlement discussion.

**Annual credit report** — The full, free copy of what each nationwide bureau holds about you, obtainable weekly at the only official site, [annualcreditreport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com). Explained in [chapter 01](01-pull-your-reports-safely.md).

**APR (annual percentage rate)** — The yearly cost of a debt, expressed as a percentage. Not the same as the interest rate: APR folds in certain specified fees too. Used throughout [chapter 05](05-debt-payoff-order.md).

**Avalanche method** — Paying debts in descending order of interest rate, after minimums, for the mathematically cheapest result. Introduced in [chapter 05](05-debt-payoff-order.md).

**Balance transfer** — Moving a balance to another card, often with a promotional low rate. Fine tool; a lifestyle is harder. Reviewed in [chapter 05](05-debt-payoff-order.md).

**Charged off** — What a creditor calls an account it has stopped expecting to be paid (usually no payment for about 180 days). It is *not* the debt being forgiven or closed — it just changed hands and can chase you in collections. Appears throughout.

**CPN ("credit privacy number")** — A number sold in a scam as a "new credit identity." Not a legal substitute for your SSN on credit applications; using one that way is a federal crime. Defined with context in [chapter 07](07-spotting-credit-and-debt-scams.md).

**Credit bureau / reporting agency** — A company that collects and sells credit data about people. The three nationwide bureaus are Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion; a fourth, Innovis, is also worth freezing. [Chapter 01](01-pull-your-reports-safely.md) and [03](03-freezes-beat-monitoring.md).

**Credit report** — The underlying file: every account, every limit, every late month, from one bureau. Fix the report; the score follows. — [chapter 04](04-how-scores-work.md).

**Credit score** — A number computed by a formula run on report data. Many models, versions, and bureau inputs exist; no single "the" score exists. [Chapter 04](04-how-scores-work.md) has the diagram.

**Credit freeze / security freeze** — The statutory, free tool that blocks anyone from opening *new* credit in your name until you lift it. Stronger than monitoring. [Chapter 03](03-freezes-beat-monitoring.md) is the whole case for it.

## D–F

**Data furnisher** — The company that *reports* to the bureaus: your bank, card issuer, loan servicer, collection agency. You can dispute bad data directly to them too — [chapter 02](02-dispute-errors-plain-letters.md) and the [templates](dispute-letter-templates.md).

**Debt management plan (DMP)** — A schedule run through nonprofit counseling agencies where creditors agree to reduced rates and you pay one agency payment monthly. Covered soberly in [chapter 05](05-debt-payoff-order.md).

**Debt settlement** — Paying less than owed, usually in one negotiated lump. Real tool, real costs; can be tax-relevant; slow; damages your credit in flight. [Chapter 07](07-spotting-credit-and-debt-scams.md).

**Debt validation** — A collector's obligation to show *in writing* that the debt is real, that you owe it, and that they have the right to collect it. Free, yours, by law. [Chapter 06](06-collector-call-scripts.md) — and the template.

**Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA)** — The federal law governing credit reports, bureaus, and disputes: it gives you the right to a free weekly report, the right to dispute, and gives bureaus roughly 30 days to investigate. Backbone of [chapters 01](01-pull-your-reports-safely.md)–[03](03-freezes-beat-monitoring.md).

**Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA)** — The federal law governing what third-party debt collectors can and cannot do — call times, harassment, third-party disclosure, written validation. Behind all of [chapter 06](06-collector-call-scripts.md).

**FICO / VantageScore** — Two major *families* of scoring models. Score bodies differ, so your number shifts a little by model and version — which is normal, not fraud. [Chapter 04](04-how-scores-work.md).

**Fraud alert** — A flag lenders should see and take a second look before opening anything new; good alongside a freeze, especially after identity theft. Decrypted in [chapter 03](03-freezes-beat-monitoring.md).

## H–P

**Hard inquiry** — A credit pull a lender performs because you applied for credit; can nick a score briefly. [Chapter 01](01-pull-your-reports-safely.md).

**Hardship program** — An issuer's internal plan for people in rough months: lower rates, skipped payments. Quietly offered; costs nothing to ask. [Chapter 05](05-debt-payoff-order.md).

**Nonprofit credit counseling** — Actual help with budgeting, hardship plans, and debt-management setup, from organizations with a real tax-exempt mission. — [chapter 05](05-debt-payoff-order.md); vet in [chapter 07](07-spotting-credit-and-debt-scams.md).

**Payday rollover** — Paying a fee to extend a too-short payday loan, again and again. The fee engine is the product. Pointed away from gently in [chapters 05](05-debt-payoff-order.md) and [07](07-spotting-credit-and-debt-scams.md).

## R–U

**Re-insertion** — When a deleted disputed item returns to your report; the bureau is generally supposed to notify you within about 5 business days when it does. [Chapter 02](02-dispute-errors-plain-letters.md).

**Snowball method** — Paying debts smallest-balance-first, buying speed-of-victory instead of minimum-interest; costlier, more durable. Honored alongside avalanche in [chapter 05](05-debt-payoff-order.md).

**Soft inquiry** — Any credit pull *not* tied to an application: your own checks, preexisting obligations, employers you consented to. Never touches your score. [Chapter 01](01-pull-your-reports-safely.md).

**Statute of limitations (debt)** — The number of years a collector can successfully use the courts over a debt, which varies by state — and which a partial payment or a written acknowledgment can restart in many states. [Chapter 06](06-collector-call-scripts.md); state AG to verify.

**Tradeline** — The report-speak name for a single account row on a credit report. Used throughout [chapter 01](01-pull-your-reports-safely.md).

**Utilization** — The percentage of your credit limit you're using — measured per card and overall, usually from the statement balance. One of the two biggest score factors. [Chapter 04](04-how-scores-work.md).

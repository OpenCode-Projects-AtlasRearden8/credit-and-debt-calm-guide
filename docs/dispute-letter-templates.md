# Dispute letter templates

*Reading time: about 6 minutes. This is the copy-and-fill companion to [chapter 02](02-dispute-errors-plain-letters.md).*

Three letters below, each one page, each built the same calm way: facts, ask, enclosure, done. Replace everything in `[brackets]`, delete anything that doesn't apply, and never include original documents — **copies only**. Keep a copy of every letter and enclosure in the folder where your reports live (a phone photo of the sealed, certified envelope is the poor person's notary).

Print-button is up top; these pages are meant to be printed.

## Letter 1 — dispute to a credit bureau

Use when: something is wrong on a report from **Equifax, Experian, or TransUnion**. Send to the bureau's *disputes* address — check each bureau's current dispute mailing address on its own website before mailing, because these move occasionally. Certified mail, return receipt requested.

```text
[Your full name]
[Your mailing address]
[City, State ZIP]
[Date]

[Credit bureau name]
[Their current disputes address — check their website]

Re: Dispute of inaccurate information — [your name], SSN ending in [last 4 only]

To whom it may concern:

I am disputing the following item(s) reported in my credit file. Please
investigate and correct or delete each item, and send me written notice
of the results within the statutory period.

Item 1: [Account name and number as it appears on the report]
- The report shows: [what the report says, e.g. "reported limit $1,000"]
- The correct information is: [what it should say, e.g. "actual limit $4,000,
  per the enclosed May statement"]
- What I am asking for: [correction / deletion / removal as obsolete]

Item 2: [same structure; one ask per item]

Enclosures: [copies of] proof of the correct information [statement, letter,
agency report]; copy of my photo ID; copy of a utility bill showing my address.

Thank you for your prompt attention.

Sincerely,
[Signature]
[Printed name]
```

Notes: the "what's right instead" line is what makes the letter short and checkable. If you don't know the correct value, say what it is *not* and ask them to obtain and confirm it. If this follows an identity-theft report, mention "Enclosed: FTC identity theft report from identitytheft.gov."

## Letter 2 — direct dispute to the creditor or lender

Use when: the company that *reported* the bad data is the creditor or lender itself. You can dispute to them directly under the same law — sometimes they fix their own feed faster than the bureaus do. Use the same structure as Letter 1, addressed to the creditor's **credit disputes / customer relations** address (their website lists it; billing statements sometimes have a disputes address — mail to the address specified for reporting disputes, not the payment address).

```text
[Your full name]
[Your mailing address]
[City, State ZIP]
[Date]

[Creditor name]
[Their disputes address]

Re: Dispute of inaccurate information — account [number]

To whom it may concern:

Your company reports the account above inaccurately to the consumer
reporting agencies. Specifically:

- The report shows: [the wrong detail]
- The correct information is: [the true detail, and what proves it]
- What I am asking you to do: correct the information you report
  about this account with Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, and
  confirm the correction to me in writing.

Enclosures: [copies of proof].

Thank you.

Sincerely,
[Signature]
[Printed name]
```

## Letter 3 — debt validation request (to a collector)

Use when: a debt collector first contacts you (and within about 30 days of the collection notice if one arrived — see [chapter 06](06-collector-call-scripts.md) for why that window matters). Certified mail, return receipt.

```text
[Your full name]
[Your mailing address]
[City, State ZIP]
[Date]

[Collection agency name]
[Their address from their notice or website]

Re: Disputed debt — [reference number from their letters if you have one]

To whom it may concern:

I dispute this alleged debt and request validation. Please provide, in writing:

1. The amount claimed, and a breakdown of any interest and fees added;
2. The name and address of the original creditor;
3. Copies of documents showing that I owe the debt
   (such as the original account agreement and account statements);
4. Proof that your company has the right to collect this debt
   (for example, an assignment, purchase agreement, or similar).

Until you have provided this verification, please treat this letter as a
request that all communications with me be in writing.

Thank you.

Sincerely,
[Signature]
[Printed name]
```

Notes: this letter does not admit the debt (read it — it never says "mine"), does not promise payment, and puts everything in writing, exactly like the scripts in [chapter 06](06-collector-call-scripts.md). If they can't validate, that's their problem to solve; whether a partially paid old debt restarts your state's clock is answered by your state Attorney General, not by this template.

## The 30-day rhythm

Once a letter is mailed (certified, keep the receipt):

- [ ] Log it: date, bureau/creditor/agency, item(s), receipt number — [tracker worksheet](worksheet-dispute-tracker.md)
- [ ] Calendar the due date: mailing date + 35 days (a small pad for their extra few days)
- [ ] Read the reply: deleted, corrected, verified, other
- [ ] If "verified" and you still believe it's wrong: re-dispute once with *new specific evidence*
- [ ] If they've ignored deadlines or the item re-inserted: CFPB complaint, free, at [consumerfinance.gov/complaint](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/complaint/)

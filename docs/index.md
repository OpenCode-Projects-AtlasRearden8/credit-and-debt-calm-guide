# Welcome — start here

*Reading time: about 5 minutes. Chapters take 6 to 10 minutes each.*

This is a guide about credit and debt, written the way a patient friend would explain it: plainly, step by step, and without making you feel one inch worse about where you're starting. Maybe your credit is fine and you just want to understand it. Maybe an old medical bill is still haunting your report. Maybe collectors are calling and you don't know what to say — or what *not* to say.

Wherever you're starting, you are not behind, and you are not in trouble. There is no "wrong" position, only a current position. This guide helps you move from it, steadily, using paperwork and patience instead of fear and money you don't have.

## A promise before we start

- **No shame.** Debt happens to normal people doing normal things: paying for school, a car, a layoff, a hospital visit. A credit report is not a character report.
- **No scare tactics.** You will not be told your life is ruined. It isn't.
- **No jargon walls.** Every term gets a plain-English definition, and the heavy ones live in the [glossary](glossary.md).
- **No payments to anyone in here.** Everything in this guide is free, do-it-yourself, and legal. If a chapter ever says "hire help," it will also tell you how to vet that help first.
- **No magic.** There is no trick that deletes accurate information faster than time does. Anyone promising one is covered in [chapter 07](07-spotting-credit-and-debt-scams.md).

## The three moves that carry most of the weight

If you read nothing else, these three cover a surprising amount of ground, in about the order you should do them:

1. **Pull your own reports yourself — it never hurts your score.** You can't fix what you can't see, and your own pull is the free, safe kind. Chapter [Pull your reports safely](01-pull-your-reports-safely.md) walks you through it without typing your personal info into the wrong website.
2. **Dispute errors in plain, short letters.** Not ten pages of legal-sounding noise — a short, factual letter is a stronger dispute, and it's free anyway. Chapter [Dispute errors in plain letters](02-dispute-errors-plain-letters.md) shows exactly how, and the [letter templates](dispute-letter-templates.md) are ready to copy.
3. **Freeze your credit. Free, and stronger than any monitoring service.** A freeze makes it so nobody — including an identity thief pretending to be you — can open new accounts in your name. It takes minutes and it can't be scammed out of you. Chapter [Freezes beat monitoring](03-freezes-beat-monitoring.md) explains the logistics plainly.

Everything else — understanding scores, ordering your payoffs, handling collector calls calmly — is what you do after those three are in motion.

## How the flow goes

You can read in any order, but a sensible sequence for real life is:

1. Chapter 01 (pull reports) → read your own reports with the worksheets open.
2. Chapter 02 (disputes) + the templates + the tracker → fix anything wrong.
3. Chapter 03 (freeze) → lock the door on future trouble. One evening, done.
4. Chapter 04 (scores) → understand what you're looking at before you optimize anything.
5. Chapter 05 (payoff order) → decide *how much extra* goes *where*, one worksheet row at a time.
6. Chapter 06 (collector scripts) → keep it in the glovebox for the day a "Restricted" number calls.
7. Chapter 07 (scams) → read tonight if a stranger has offered to "fix" your credit for money.

## A friendly watch-out

This guide is **education, not advice**. Laws vary by state (especially around deadlines, debts, and contracts), your situation is its own situation, and big decisions deserve a real professional who knows your file — vetted with chapter [07](07-spotting-credit-and-debt-scams.md) first, though. When rules are state-specific — statute of limitations, garnishment, whether a payment "restarts the clock" — this guide tells you where to check official answers (CFPB, FTC, your state Attorney General) rather than guess.
